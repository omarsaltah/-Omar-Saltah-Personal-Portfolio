Chiaroscuro

An interactive field guide

Light is the first material.

Walls only hold the dark back. Five touch-and-drag experiments on how sun, shade and reflection shape the space around us.

“Architecture is the masterly, correct and magnificent play of masses brought together in light.”
Le Corbusier

01 Time

The sun draws the room

A window is a sundial. As the sun climbs and falls, the patch of light on the floor slides, shrinks and stretches. Low sun reaches deep; high sun barely gets in.

Make the sun reach the back wall.

In the world: the Pantheon’s oculus throws a disc of sun that crawls around the dome all day, a clock made of light.

02 Shade

Shape the opening

Summer sun is high, winter sun is low. A simple overhang can tell them apart: it blocks the steep summer rays and lets the shallow winter ones slip underneath.

Block the summer sun fully while the winter sun still enters.

In the world: Le Corbusier’s brise-soleil turned this one trick into a facade language.

03 Edge

Hard light, soft light

The bigger the light source, the softer the shadow’s edge. A point of sun carves a razor line; an overcast sky wraps around objects and the shadow melts. Drag the lamp.

Cast a razor-sharp shadow edge.

In the world: galleries use diffuse north light from skylights so paintings glow without harsh shadows.

04 Bounce

Surfaces carry light further

Sun on the floor becomes a second light source. A pale surface bounces it up to the ceiling and fills the room; a dark one swallows it. Polished stone redirects it like a mirror.

05 Compose

Light a plan

Now you’re the lighting designer. This is a floor plan seen from above. Drag lights and columns: shadows are real, traced from every edge. Mix warm and cool, and soften the source.

Mix a warm and a cool light in one plan.

In the world: in Tadao Ando’s Church of the Light, one cross cut through a concrete wall is the whole lighting scheme.

You read light now.

  • Time — low sun reaches deep, high sun stays near the glass.
  • Shade — geometry can filter seasons.
  • Edge — bigger sources, softer shadows.
  • Bounce — surfaces redistribute light.
  • Compose — place, colour and soften to set a mood.
